Essential question

Why is the number on your paycheck smaller than the number you expected?

Today I can…
  • Read & listen: find the number that answers a question on a pay stub, and explain the difference between gross pay and net pay, naming two deductions.
  • Speak: say where the money went in a full sentence: “___ percent of the check goes to ___.”
  • Write: answer the essential question in an O·R·E·A·C paragraph with at least three vocabulary words and two sentences with a percentage or $X of the $Y.

    Objectives (full)

    Content (ELA + personal finance)

    • Locate details in a text and a sample pay stub to explain the difference between gross pay and net pay, naming two deductions.

    Marisol worked 20 hours at a grocery store in Laurel. Her pay was $12 an hour, so she did the math in her head on the bus home: $240. When she opened the , the check said $204.65. Nobody had made a mistake. The difference between what you and what you is the first lesson every worker learns, and it is easier to accept when you know where the money went.

    Marisol trabajó 20 horas en una tienda de comestibles en Laurel. Le pagaban $12 por hora, así que hizo la cuenta mentalmente en el autobús de regreso: $240. Cuando abrió el sobre, el cheque decía $204.65. Nadie se había equivocado. La diferencia entre lo que ganas y lo que te llevas a casa es la primera lección que aprende todo trabajador, y es más fácil aceptarla cuando sabes a dónde fue el dinero.
    2

    The big number is called . It is hours times , before anything is taken out: 20 hours times $12 equals $240. The smaller number is , the amount that actually reaches your hand or your bank account. Everything between gross and net is called a : money before you are paid. On most , the deductions are printed in a column with their own names, and the names look like a until someone explains them.

    El número grande se llama salario bruto. Son las horas por la tarifa, antes de que se descuente nada: 20 horas por $12 son $240. El número más pequeño es el salario neto, la cantidad que de verdad llega a tu mano o a tu cuenta bancaria. Todo lo que hay entre el bruto y el neto se llama deducción: dinero retenido antes de que te paguen. En la mayoría de los talones de pago, las deducciones aparecen en una columna con sus propios nombres, y los nombres parecen un código secreto hasta que alguien los explica.

Every worker pays 7.65 of every check into FICA, no matter how young, and the pays the same amount again. Unlike federal income tax, you do not get FICA back in the spring.     Grammar move · Numbers in sentences Movida de gramática · Números en oraciones

    Grammar move · 7 minutes Movida de gramática · 7 minutos
    7:00

    A pay stub is full of numbers, and a good sentence puts each number to work. Two patterns: number + percent of + noun + singular verb (7.65 percent of every check goes to FICA) and $X of the $Y + was ($18.36 of the $240 was withheld). Say the decimal out loud: seven point six five. Tap a button to build each sentence, then read it aloud.Un talón de pago está lleno de números, y una buena oración pone a trabajar cada número. Dos patrones: número + percent of + sustantivo + verbo en singular (7.65 percent of every check goes to FICA) y $X of the $Y + was ($18.36 of the $240 was withheld). Di el decimal en voz alta: seven point six five. Toca un botón para formar cada oración y luego léela en voz alta.

    1. FICA  →  7.65%  ·  $18.36 of $240

    2. Federal income tax  →  4%  ·  $9.60 of $240

    3. Take-home pay  →  about 85%  ·  $204.65 of $240
